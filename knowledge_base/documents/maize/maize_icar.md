# Maize Agricultural Knowledge

## Source
Organization: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR-IIMR) & ANGRAU
Source: ICAR - Indian Institute of Maize Research / ANGRAU Production Guidelines

## Crop Suitability
Maize (Zea mays) is a high-yielding C4 cereal crop with broad agro-climatic adaptability.
Optimal temperature is 18°C to 35°C during the growing season.
Grows best on deep, fertile, well-drained loamy, red, and alluvial soils rich in organic matter. Sensitive to salinity and waterlogging.

## Season and Sowing
- Kharif: Sowing in June–July with pre-monsoon rains.
- Rabi: Sowing in October–November. Rabi maize typically produces higher yields due to clear sunshine days and lower pest pressure.

## Water and Irrigation
Medium water requirement (500–1000 mm).
Critical moisture stages: knee-high stage, tasseling, silking, and grain filling. Drought stress during silking causes severe yield drop.

## Production and Economic Guidance
- Expected yield: 2,200–2,800 kg/acre (22–28 quintals/acre).
- Cultivation cost: ₹22,000–₹26,000 per acre (requires adequate nitrogen and phosphorus fertilization).
- High demand from starch and poultry feed industries ensures steady mandi liquidation.

## Recommendation Principle
Recommended for farmers with medium water availability, fertile loamy or red soils, seeking reliable yield and steady commercial absorption without extreme vegetable price volatility.
