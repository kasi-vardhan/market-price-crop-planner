# Black Gram (Urad Dal) — Production Guide for Andhra Pradesh

**Source:** ICAR-Indian Institute of Pulses Research (IIPR), Kanpur, and ANGRAU (Acharya N.G. Ranga Agricultural University) Regional Agricultural Research Station, Andhra Pradesh.

**Geographic Applicability:** Andhra Pradesh — Coastal Andhra, Rayalaseema, and North Andhra districts.

---

## Overview

Black gram (*Vigna mungo*), also known as urad dal or minumulu in Telugu, is a short-duration pulse crop widely grown in Andhra Pradesh. It is valued for its high protein content, nitrogen fixation capacity, and relatively low cultivation cost. The crop is suitable for both kharif and rabi seasons and can be grown on a variety of soils.

---

## Suitable Soils

Black gram grows best on:
- Red soils (laterite): well-drained, preferred in Rayalaseema and coastal areas
- Loamy soils: good water retention with adequate drainage
- Black cotton soils (vertisols): suitable for rabi season under residual moisture
- Alluvial soils: suitable in coastal Andhra

The crop is sensitive to waterlogging. Soils with good drainage are essential. Avoid heavy clay or poorly drained soils.

---

## Climate Requirements

- **Temperature:** 22°C to 35°C optimal. The crop does not tolerate frost.
- **Rainfall:** 400–900 mm per season is adequate. Waterlogging is harmful.
- **Season:**
  - *Kharif* (June–September): main cropping season
  - *Rabi* (October–February): second season, grown on residual moisture in black soils

---

## Water Requirements

Black gram is a rainfed crop in most of Andhra Pradesh. It has low-to-medium water requirements. One or two protective irrigations during flowering and pod-filling stages significantly improve yield. Waterlogging must be avoided at all growth stages.

---

## Variety Recommendations (ANGRAU)

- **LBG 648:** Kharif, 65–70 days, tolerant to yellow mosaic virus
- **LBG 752:** Rabi, 75–80 days, high yielding
- **PU 31:** Kharif, 60–65 days, bushy plant type
- **WBG 26:** Kharif/Rabi dual purpose, 70–75 days

---

## Agronomic Practices

### Land Preparation
- Plough 2–3 times and bring to a fine tilth.
- Apply basal dose of 10 tonnes of farmyard manure (FYM) per hectare during land preparation.

### Seed Rate and Spacing
- Seed rate: 15–20 kg per acre
- Spacing: 30 cm × 10 cm (row × plant)
- Treat seeds with Rhizobium culture and phosphate solubilizing bacteria before sowing.

### Fertilizer (per acre)
- Nitrogen (N): 8 kg (as basal, to support early growth)
- Phosphorus (P₂O₅): 16 kg
- Potassium (K₂O): 8 kg
- Apply all fertilizer as basal dose at time of sowing.

---

## Yield Expectations

- **Average yield:** 300–450 kg per acre (rainfed)
- **Improved variety with good management:** up to 500–600 kg per acre
- **ANGRAU benchmark for AP:** 450 kg per acre (used for financial projections)

*Note: Actual yields vary with rainfall, soil fertility, variety, and pest management. The 450 kg/acre figure used in this system's financial calculations is based on ANGRAU state averages and should be treated as an estimate, not a guarantee.*

## Pest and Disease Management

### Major Pests
- **Aphids** and **thrips**: treat with imidacloprid (0.3 ml/litre) or neem-based sprays
- **Pod borer** (*Maruca vitrata*): spray monocrotophos at pod formation

### Major Diseases
- **Yellow Mosaic Virus (YMV)**: the most destructive disease; use resistant varieties (LBG 648); control whitefly vector
- **Cercospora leaf spot**: apply mancozeb at 10-day intervals
- **Root rot**: ensure proper drainage; avoid waterlogging

---

## Post-Harvest and Marketing

- Harvest when 80% of pods turn black/mature.
- Thresh and dry to 10–12% moisture content.
- Mandi prices for black gram in AP: Rs 70–90 per kg (AGMARKNET, Kakinada, 2026).
- Major markets: Kakinada (East Godavari), Eluru (West Godavari), Nellore.

---

## Risks and Limitations

1. **Yellow Mosaic Virus** can cause 70–100% crop loss if susceptible varieties are used.
2. Excess rainfall or waterlogging causes root rot and crop failure.
3. Market prices for black gram are volatile; check mandi prices before sowing.
4. Kharif crop faces risk from late-season rains at harvest time.
