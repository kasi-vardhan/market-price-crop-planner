# Green Gram (Moong) Agricultural Knowledge

## Source
Organization: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR-IIPR) & ANGRAU
Source: ICAR - Indian Institute of Pulses Research / ANGRAU Pulses Advisory Manual

## Crop Suitability
Green gram (Vigna radiata) is a short-duration pulse crop (60–75 days) well adapted to tropical and subtropical climates.
Optimal growing temperature is between 22°C and 35°C.
It thrives in well-drained loamy, sandy-loam, and red soils with a neutral pH (6.5–7.5). Waterlogged soils must be avoided as the crop is highly sensitive to poor aeration.

## Season and Sowing
- Kharif: Sowing in June to July with onset of monsoon.
- Rabi: Sowing in October to November in rice fallows with residual moisture.
- Zaid (Summer): Sowing in late February to March under light assured irrigation.

## Water and Irrigation
Green gram has low to medium water requirement (400–800 mm).
It requires 2 to 3 light irrigations at critical stages: branching and pod formation. Excessive water or heavy continuous rainfall during maturity causes pod shattering and grain damage.

## Production and Economic Guidance
- Average expected yield under good management: 450–600 kg/acre.
- Estimated cultivation cost: ₹15,000–₹18,000 per acre (low input requirement due to atmospheric nitrogen fixation).
- Low capital risk compared to commercial vegetables, making it suitable for budget-constrained farmers.

## Recommendation Principle
Green gram is highly recommended as a low-risk, short-duration pulse for light to medium soils with low water availability or as a catch crop after kharif harvest.
