# Bengal Gram (Chickpea / Chana) Agricultural Knowledge

## Source
Organization: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR-IIPR) & PJTSAU
Source: ICAR - Indian Institute of Pulses Research / PJTSAU Package of Practices

## Crop Suitability
Bengal gram (Cicer arietinum) is the premier rabi pulse crop of Central and Southern India.
It requires a cool, dry climate during vegetative growth and warm conditions during maturity.
Optimal temperature is 15°C to 28°C. Extreme heat during flowering causes floral abortion.
It is best suited for heavy black cotton soils, clay loams, and deep loamy soils with good moisture retention capacity.

## Season and Sowing
- Exclusively a Rabi season crop (October to mid-November sowing).
- Sowing into receding monsoon moisture is standard in rainfed regions (Rayalaseema and Northern Karnataka).

## Water and Irrigation
Low water requirement (350–700 mm).
Primarily grown on conserved soil moisture. In irrigated areas, 1–2 critical irrigations (pre-flowering and pod development) boost yield substantially. Avoid irrigation during peak flowering.

## Production and Economic Guidance
- Expected yield: 700–900 kg/acre on fertile black soils; 500–650 kg/acre on rainfed light soils.
- Cultivation cost: ₹16,000–₹20,000 per acre.
- Market stability is generally high due to nationwide consumption and minimum support price (MSP) backing.

## Recommendation Principle
Ideal crop for rabi season in black and clay soils with low to medium water availability. Does not perform well in poorly drained or waterlogged soils.
