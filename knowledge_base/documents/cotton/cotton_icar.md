# Cotton Agricultural Knowledge

## Source
Organization: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR-CICR) & ANGRAU
Source: ICAR - Central Institute for Cotton Research / ANGRAU Commercial Crops Guide

## Crop Suitability
Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum / arboreum) is a major commercial cash crop in Deccan and Central India.
Optimal temperature is 21°C to 36°C. Clear bright days during flowering and boll opening are essential.
Grows best in deep black cotton soils (Vertisols) and fertile alluvial loams with good water retention and drainage. Highly sensitive to frost and waterlogging during early seedling stages.

## Season and Sowing
- Kharif season crop: Sown in late June to July with onset of monsoon.
- Duration: 150–180 days.

## Water and Irrigation
Medium to high water requirement (600–1100 mm).
Critical water stages: square formation, flowering, and boll development. Moisture stress during flowering causes heavy shedding of flowers and squares.

## Production and Economic Guidance
- Expected yield: 800–1,100 kg/acre (seed cotton / kapas).
- Cultivation cost: ₹34,000–₹40,000 per acre (higher expenditure on pest management like pink bollworm, quality hybrid seed, and labour for harvesting/picking).
- High gross revenue potential but carries higher production cost and price volatility risk.

## Recommendation Principle
Recommended for farmers with medium-to-large capital budget, fertile black or deep loamy soils, and experience in intensive pest scouting and moisture management.
